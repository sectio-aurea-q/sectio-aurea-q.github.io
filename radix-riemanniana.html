The Radix Riemanniana

Optimal windowed test functions for bounding analytic ranks of elliptic curves
Sabrina Hackfort · Independent Researcher · ORCID 0009-0007-7458-2487
❦ ❦ ❦

The explicit formula connects the zeros of an L-function with the primes. If one looks only through a window [−L, L], a finite piece of prime data is enough to say something exact about the zeros. The Radix Riemanniana is the root of that window: the reproducing kernel of the Weil form, built from the Gamma factor, the conductor and the primes alone, without knowing a single zero.

Definition — Radix Riemanniana (Hackfort, 2026)

For an elliptic curve E over ℚ and a window [−L, L], with G the Gram matrix of the Weil form of E and Φ(z) the Fourier basis on the window,

ℛE,L(w, z) = Φ(w)* G−1 Φ(z).

What it solves

How large is the rank of an elliptic curve? This question lies behind the conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer. Assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, the explicit formula bounds the analytic rank from the traces ap. Until now this bound was computed with a fixed test function. The Radix Riemanniana yields the best possible one in the window, separately for every curve:

ran(E) ≤ 1 / ℛE,L(0, 0)
under GRH · from the primes p < e2L alone · never weaker than the fixed kernel

The Radix in three dimensions

How it is checked

Every bound is certified with interval arithmetic (Arb), so rounding cannot produce a false result. As an independent cross-check, the same quantity is computed a second way, from the actual zeros of L(E, s). Both agree, and the gap halves with each doubling of the zeros included. Of more than fifteen thousand certified bounds, not one falls below the known rank.

What it does not claim

The bounds assume GRH and concern the analytic rank. Weil Gram matrices as such are known (Yoshida; Connes–Consani–Moscovici; Suzuki; Shi). What is new is the per-curve optimisation from the actual ap, its certification, and the systematic comparison. Curves of record rank have not yet been tested.
