Tunnelling Informatics

The data-driven study of hydrogen tunnelling in enzymes
Sabrina Hackfort · Independent Researcher · ORCID 0009-0007-7458-2487
❦ ❦ ❦

Not to be confused with the information systems of tunnel construction, nor with network tunnelling in computer science, such as VPN or SSH tunnels. The tunnel here is the quantum-mechanical one.

Enzymes move hydrogen — as a proton, as a hydride ion, as a hydrogen atom — and part of it often does not climb over the energy barrier of the reaction but passes through it. Since 1989, when Cha, Murray and Klinman reported isotope effects in yeast alcohol dehydrogenase that pointed to tunnelling, this has been read mainly from kinetic isotope effects: how much slower a reaction runs when deuterium or tritium takes the place of hydrogen, and how that ratio changes with temperature. These numbers are scattered over hundreds of papers, written in different conventions, and often cited on without their conditions. Tunnelling informatics treats them as data.

Definition

Tunnelling informatics (also tunneling informatics) is the data-driven study of hydrogen tunnelling in enzymes. It collects the measured kinetic isotope effects with their sources and conditions, establishes what such data can and cannot prove about tunnelling, re-examines the published claims against them, and tests predictions made from enzyme structure. Every conclusion is to follow from open data and from rules stated in advance.

The name was introduced by Sabrina Hackfort in 2026.

Four Parts

I Data  ·  II Proof  ·  III Re-examination  ·  IV Prediction
  1. The data. MEG-NEN, a curated database of the measured kinetic isotope effects of enzymes: every value with its source, isotopes, temperature, pH and conventions, and the sign of every difference in activation energy checked against the paper's own equation. Each value is read twice, independently, before it is accepted. Values that a paper only cites from other work, or shows only in a figure or its supporting information, are left out.
  2. What the data can prove. An identifiability result: which statements about tunnelling such measurements can establish, and which no number of them can. An isotope effect is no direct measure of tunnelling, since zero-point energy alone already makes hydrogen react faster than deuterium (see ZENO).
  3. The re-examination. The claims made since 1989, checked against the data: where the measurements support them, where they contradict them, and where they cannot decide.
  4. The prediction. Predictions from an enzyme's structure, made before the measurement and then confirmed or refuted in the laboratory. A first structure-based classifier, MEG-APSU, exists; its honest accuracy and its limits are on its page.

Where It Stands

On 8 November 2026, MEG-NEN held 189 kinetic isotope effects and 88 temperature series from 37 papers, each value read twice. It is in construction and not yet public; it will be released with its documentation. The identifiability result, the re-examination and the prediction build on it, in that order.

Related Work

  • The Hackfort Equation — the quantum fraction η = 1 − 1/κ, the part of a rate that exists only because of tunnelling.
  • The tunnelling fraction of a reaction rate: conventions, interpretation and certified reference values — preprint, October 2026.
  • ELIAH — the Eckart tunnelling coefficient κ, computed accurately where the textbook formula fails and checked against 1197 certified values.
  • ZENO — separates the tunnelling fraction of an enzyme reaction from its isotope effect.
  • MEG-APSU — classifies an enzyme from its structure as quantum-critical or classical.
  • TERRA — a survey of critically endangered species for enzymes that rely on tunnelling, built on MEG-APSU.
