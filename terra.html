Terra

What quantum machinery is lost when a species is lost — a survey, not an inventory
Sabrina Hackfort · Independent Researcher · ORCID 0009-0007-7458-2487
❦ ❦ ❦

Some enzymes do their work by letting a hydrogen pass straight through an energy barrier instead of climbing over it — a quantum tunnel. TERRA asks a simple question across the edge of extinction: among the species we are about to lose, how many carry enzymes that rely on this tunnel? It does not measure new chemistry in the laboratory. It reads what is already known, species by species, and sorts it.

The pipeline
IUCN Red List → enzymes via UniProt → structures from the PDB → classification by the MEG-APSU Lindblad solver

Every one of the 10,774 species currently listed as Critically Endangered was put through this pipeline. For most of them the public databases simply hold no enzyme data: only 2,274 species returned enzymes at all. That gap is part of the result and is stated here plainly, not hidden.

The Numbers, With Their Denominators

A single percentage means nothing without saying what it is divided by. TERRA found 1,424 quantum-critical enzymes. Depending on what one counts them against, the rate looks very different — and the honest reading is the lower one.

TERRA v0.5.0, full run. Richest single species: Asarcornis scutulata, the white-winged duck, with 9 quantum-critical enzymes.

The point that survives all three denominators is this: quantum-critical enzymes are not confined to a few groups. They turn up spread across the breadth of the endangered list. No branch of the tree looks quantum-expendable.

What It Does Not Claim

TERRA is a survey, not a complete inventory, and not the first word on the subject. For roughly 8,500 of the listed species no enzyme data existed to scan, so the true distribution may differ once those gaps are filled. The classification rests on the MEG-APSU model, whose own accuracy is quoted on its page and is not perfect. An earlier version of this page reported a single rate without naming its denominator and spoke of a constant share near 18 %; the real rates are 8.8 % per enzyme and 11.3 % per species, and that claim is withdrawn.
